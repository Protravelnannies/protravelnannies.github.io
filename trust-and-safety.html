Trust & Safety

Trust goes both ways.

If you're a parent, you want to know who's looking after your children. If you're a nanny, you want to know whose home you're walking into. And everyone wants to know what happens if something goes wrong. This page explains, in plain language, what we actually do about all of that.

What we check Booking policies

Our approach

The questions we try to answer before anyone says yes

Most childcare services only worry about the first one. We built our process around all three, because children are better looked after when the parents and the nanny both know what's going on and have someone to call.

For families

"Who am I trusting with my children?"

Verified nanny profiles, with each check explained, an intro video, and a nanny matched to what your children actually need.

For nannies

"Who am I working for?"

Verified families, correct information about the children, and a written brief before you accept anything.

For everyone

"What if something goes wrong?"

Clear booking terms, an emergency procedure, incident reports based on facts, and a real person you can contact.

Nanny verification

What we check, and what the badges mean

We don't use one vague "verified" label. Nanny profiles show separate badges instead, and each one means a specific thing has been checked. That way you can see exactly what we've looked at.

Identity verified

We've checked the nanny's identity against official documents and, where it applies, their right to work. The documents themselves stay private and never appear on a profile.

Video interview completed

We've talked to them face to face on video about their experience, how they think about safety and what they understand the job to be, and made sure they're the same person as on the application.

References checked

We've spoken to their referees ourselves, following our reference procedure. We ask about reliability, the children's ages, what the nanny was responsible for and whether they'd hire them again. We don't just collect names on a form.

Experience verified

We've confirmed the childcare experience on the profile, including the age groups, using references and supporting information.

First aid verifiedQualification verified

We've seen proof of a current certificate or qualification, for example first aid, CPR, early-years, newborn care or water-safety training. If a certificate expires, it has to be renewed for the badge to stay.

Sex-offence certificate checked

In Spain, anyone who works with children must hold a sex-offence certificate by law (Certificado de Delitos de Naturaleza Sexual). We check the original for each nanny before their first booking and show the date we checked it. Nannies who aren't Spanish nationals also give us the equivalent from their home country. Nobody works without one.

Profile reviewed

We've looked over the nanny's photo, intro video and written profile against our standards before putting it live.

Self-declared vs. verified skills

Some skills, like "confident swimmer", may be the nanny's own word. A verified badge only goes on formal qualifications we've actually seen, such as water-safety training or a lifeguard qualification. Profiles show which is which.

Checks lower the risk. They can't take it away completely. A badge tells you that one clearly defined check was done, and it doesn't claim anything beyond that. We also keep checks up to date. References get refreshed, certificates get renewed, and feedback after each booking feeds into our ongoing quality review.

Family verification

Nannies need to feel sure too.

If you're walking into a stranger's home or flying abroad with a family, you should know who they are. So families go through verification as well, and nannies can see which steps are done before they accept.

I've been the nanny on a booking where the information turned out to be wrong. Checking families isn't about suspecting anyone. It just means everybody starts with the same correct, agreed details, and the children are better cared for because of it.

What families complete
  • Identity verified: the person booking really is who they say they are.
  • Contact details verified: we can reach the parents before and during the booking.
  • Payment verified: payment is secured through us before anything's confirmed.
  • Children's details completed: correct ages, needs and anything else relevant.
  • Booking brief confirmed: hours, location, duties and expectations, agreed in writing.
  • Accommodation details: verified where appropriate, such as for longer or travel bookings.

Profile visibility & privacy

Who sees what, and when

Profiles help families and nannies get to know each other, but that doesn't mean everyone can see your personal details. We share things bit by bit, as they're needed.

  1. Public information

    Not much, and the person chooses it: a first name, an approved photo, a short intro and a few skills. Children's photos are never made public by default.

  2. Matched profile

    When we suggest a match, the family sees the nanny's full profile, video and badges. The nanny sees the family profile, the children's ages and interests, and the booking brief.

  3. Confirmed booking information

    Phone numbers, emails, exact addresses and sensitive things like allergies are only shared once a booking is confirmed, and only with the people who need them.

  4. Private records

    ID documents, background-check records, full reference notes and bank details stay with us and never appear on a profile.

Safety standards

What our nannies sign up to

Nannies go through these written standards before they go live. They're there so families know what to expect, and so nannies have something to point to when they need to hold a boundary.

Phone use

Phones are for messaging parents, emergencies and booking arrangements. No scrolling while the children need you.

Photos of children

Children's privacy comes first. No photos without the parents' permission, and nothing on social media or used for promotion unless they've clearly said yes.

Alcohol & substances

No alcohol or substances while working, even at weddings and events where the grown-ups are having a drink.

Water safety

The brief says whether the children can swim and whether there'll be swimming. If a family wants a lot of time in the water, we match them with a nanny whose relevant skills are clearly labelled.

Transport & driving

We never assume a nanny will drive. If it's agreed, we expect a valid licence, proper insurance, the right child seats and the family's written consent.

Professional boundaries

No visitors without permission. What happens in the family stays private. Physical boundaries are appropriate, and behaviour is handled calmly and kindly.

Medication & allergies

Parents share allergies and clear instructions before the booking. Medication is only given if the parents have put it in writing, and only if it's something the nanny is competent to do.

Arrival safety check

We encourage nannies to have a quick look round an unfamiliar hotel or villa, checking pools, balconies, stairs and doors. Families mention any risks they know about in the brief.

Reporting concerns

Our nannies know how to report a worry about a child's wellbeing, and how to speak up if they feel unsafe themselves.

And what we ask of families

Keeping things safe can't all fall on the nanny. Before their first booking, families agree to a few fair, simple things.

  • Give us accurate information, including relevant medical details and any illness
  • Provide emergency contacts and a safe environment
  • Come back at the agreed time, or let us know early if you can't
  • Don't add extra children or change duties without agreeing it first

If something happens

Our emergency procedure

The child's safety comes first. No booking, fee or business reason ever changes that. Our nannies are trained on a simple framework, so they know what to do and in what order.

Immediate danger
  • Protect
  • Emergency services
  • Parents
  • Us
  • Document

Keep the child safe, call emergency services if needed, then the parent or guardian, then us. Afterwards, write down what happened, sticking to the facts.

Non-urgent incident
  • Child safe
  • Parents
  • Us
  • Document

Check the child is safe and comfortable, tell the parents, contact us if it's appropriate, and make a note of what happened.

Before each booking, the nanny gets the emergency information they'd reasonably need. That's the parents' and a backup contact's details, the accommodation address, relevant medical and allergy information, and who's allowed to collect the children.

Incidents & disputes

The child comes first. After that, we hear both sides and look at the facts.

If something goes wrong, we don't take the family's side or the nanny's side by default. We listen to both of you and look at what really happened.

  1. Issue reported

    A family or nanny gets in touch. Serious incidents are written up with facts, not guesses.

  2. Information reviewed

    We go through the booking brief, the messages and any incident report.

  3. Both sides heard

    We speak to the family and the nanny separately, and calmly.

  4. Resolution

    We sort it out based on the terms everyone agreed to, and keep a confidential record. Anything serious goes straight into an internal review.

Data protection

Looking after your information

We're based in Spain and follow European data-protection law (GDPR). Childcare means handling sensitive information, much of it about children, so we're extra careful with it.

Read our privacy policy
  • Only what's needed. We only collect the information we need to provide safe childcare.
  • Children's information stays private. Something like a severe allergy is shared with your nanny for the booking. It never goes on a public profile.
  • Health information is protected. Only the people who need to know see allergies, medication and medical details.
  • Documents stay with us. ID and background-check records are stored securely and kept separate from profiles.
  • You stay in control. You can ask us what we hold, why we hold it and who can see it, and use your data rights whenever you like.

Proper checks

Clearly defined checks, and we explain each one.

Professionally matched

Clear protection

Written terms for both sides, which you see before you pay.

Replacement assistance

If a nanny can't make it, we'll do everything we reasonably can to find a suitable replacement.

Someone to talk to
