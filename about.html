Meet the founder

I spent years being the nanny. Now I'm building the company I wish existed.

I'm Cameron Hein. My love for children started in South Africa with my nieces and nephews, and it has taken me from being an au pair in New Jersey, to Costa Rica, to looking after international families in Spain.

My story

Caring for children has been part of my life for as long as I can remember.

I'm South African, and that's where my love for children began: with all my nieces and nephews. I was always the one happy to look after the little ones, and by 19 I decided to take that love further and become an au pair.

What I love most about childcare is watching children grow, adapt and discover the world around them. There is something incredibly rewarding about being trusted with a child's development and seeing them learn something new from one day to the next. Their innocence, curiosity and ability to experience life for the first time are what have kept me passionate about this work.

The journey

Four countries, more than ten years, one passion

Every chapter taught me something about what families need, and what the people caring for their children need too.

Photo: Cameron's years as an au pair and nanny

  1. South Africa: where it started

    My love for children started at home, looking after my nieces and nephews. Growing up with a single mother, I also learnt early what it means for a parent to have support they can genuinely rely on.

  2. New Jersey, USA: becoming an au pair

    At 19, I moved to the USA and lived with an amazing family for almost two years, caring for a three-year-old girl and a five-year-old boy. School runs, activities, meals, lunch preparation, bedtime routines, holidays and day-to-day childcare. I became a real part of their family, and received formal au pair training through AuPairCare.

  3. Costa Rica: the journey continues

    After the USA, I moved to Costa Rica with my family and continued my childcare journey there.

  4. Spain: international families

    In Spain I completed my Interior Architecture degree and went on to work in luxury real estate. Alongside that, for around four years, I worked part-time as a nanny and babysitter, caring for international families on holiday and hearing their stories first-hand.

  5. Today: building Pro Travel Nannies

    When I recently stopped my last nanny job, something just clicked: I can do this so much better. Now I'm putting everything I've learnt into a company that treats families and nannies with the same care.

What I saw from both sides

Good intentions aren't enough without a good system.

After years in the industry, I began seeing the same problems again and again. Not because anyone was a bad person, but because nothing was in place to protect the family or the nanny when things didn't go to plan.

Inaccurate information

The "10-month-old" who was 10 years old

One booking was arranged for me to look after what I understood from the booking information to be a 10-month-old baby. When I arrived, the child was actually 10 years old. A child's age completely changes the care needed, the activities expected and whether the nanny is even the right person for the job.

So: every booking starts with a detailed childcare brief that both the family and nanny confirm.

No clear policy

A sick child, and nobody knew what should happen

During another booking, the child became very sick and was vomiting. The family asked me to leave and come back later. I'd travelled there and committed my time, and suddenly none of us knew what was fair. Those decisions shouldn't be negotiated in the middle of a stressful moment.

So: we have a clear sick-child policy that everyone sees before they book.

Reserved dates

A holiday booking that fell through just before travel

I had arranged my schedule around a holiday nanny booking and was ready to travel with the family. It fell through almost immediately before I was due to leave. For the family, plans had changed. For me, those dates were gone and other work had already been turned down.

So: cancellation terms are clear, fair and agreed up front, for both sides.

Fair pay

Being incorrectly paid, and having to fight for it

I was once incorrectly paid for my own work and had to fight to receive the amount I was actually owed. Nannies shouldn't have to chase families for money they've earned.

So: payment is handled through us, with clear terms before anyone commits.

The moment it clicked

"I can do this so much better."

When I recently stopped my last nanny job, something just clicked. I thought about my love for children, and about all the stories parents had shared with me over the years.

Stories of struggling to find nannies and babysitters they could trust. Of websites that didn't feel trustworthy. Of things falling through at the last minute, poor communication and no real system behind any of it. And I knew the nanny's side too: walking into a stranger's home, hoping it would go well.

I wanted something smooth. Somewhere parents can genuinely trust us, and where nannies feel safe joining us, not scared of meeting a new family. Something honest and truthful, where nobody has to worry that something will go wrong on a job, because the vetting is thorough and the service is genuinely great.

So I decided to build it.

So I built something better

Trust goes both ways.

I believe childcare should be built around trust going in both directions.

Parents want someone they can genuinely trust with their children, especially when they're travelling in another country. They want accurate information, professional caregivers, clear communication and someone to turn to if something goes wrong.

Nannies need those same things from families: accurate information about the children, clear expectations, reliable payment, respect for their time and protection when they commit their availability to a booking.

Families deserve to know who is looking after their children. Nannies deserve to know who they are working for. Both deserve clear agreements, professional standards, secure bookings and support when something unexpected happens.

Because I've been the nanny

I know what it feels like to walk into someone else's home and trust a family you've only just met.

Because I grew up with a single mother

I've seen the challenges families face, and how much it means to a parent to have support they can genuinely rely on.

What I've built it on

Five promises, for families and nannies alike

These aren't marketing words. Each one comes from something I wish had been in place when I was the one arriving at the door.

Verified

Both families and nannies go through appropriate verification, and every check is shown clearly.

Matched

Matched to the real needs of the children and the real strengths of the nanny, not just whoever is free.

Personal

Real people, real photos and introduction videos. You know who you're dealing with, including me.

Protected

Clear booking terms, sick-child guidance and cancellation rules agreed before anyone commits.

Supported

We stay involved from the first message to the last goodnight, and don't disappear after an introduction.

The company I wish existed

Professional enough to be trusted.
Human enough to feel personal.
Structured enough to keep families and nannies protected.
International enough to make travelling families feel at home.
