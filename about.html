Meet the founder

Childcare, from someone who's done it.

Hi, I'm Cameron. I grew up in South Africa surrounded by nieces and nephews, became an au pair in New Jersey at 19, lived in Costa Rica, and for the last few years I've been looking after holiday families here in Spain. Kids have always been my thing.

Read my story Find childcare

My story

I was always the one holding the baby.

At family get-togethers back home in South Africa, I'd always end up with the little ones. Nobody had to ask me. So when I was 19 and wondering what to do next, becoming an au pair felt like the most natural thing in the world.

What I love most is watching them figure things out. One day they can't do something, and a few days later they're showing you how it's done. Kids see everything for the first time, and being around that never gets old. Being trusted with someone's child, even for one evening, is a big deal to me. I've never stopped feeling that.

The journey

Four countries and more than ten years later

Each move taught me something new about what parents need, and about what the person looking after their kids needs too.

Photo: Cameron's years as an au pair and nanny

  1. South Africa: where it started

    My nieces and nephews were my first "job". I was raised by a single mom, so I saw up close how much it matters when a parent has someone they can actually count on.

  2. New Jersey, USA: becoming an au pair

    At 19 I moved to the US and lived with an amazing family for almost two years, looking after their three-year-old daughter and five-year-old son. I did the school runs, the activities, the meals and packed lunches, bedtime and the family holidays. By the end I wasn't just the au pair, I was part of the family. I also did my formal au pair training through AuPairCare.

  3. Costa Rica: the journey continues

    After the US, I moved to Costa Rica with my family and kept looking after children there.

  4. Spain: international families

    In Spain I studied Interior Architecture and then worked in luxury real estate. But I never stopped nannying. For about four years I looked after international families on holiday, part-time, and I heard so many of their stories along the way.

  5. Today: building Pro Travel Nannies

    When I finished my last nanny job, I remember thinking: I could do this so much better. So now I'm putting everything I've learnt into a company that looks after parents and nannies equally.

What I saw from both sides

Most families were lovely. There just wasn't a proper system.

The same things kept going wrong, and it was hardly ever anyone's fault. There was just nothing in place to look after the parents or the nanny when plans changed.

Inaccurate information

I was told 10 months. The child was 10 years old.

I was booked to look after what I understood was a 10-month-old baby. I arrived expecting a baby, and the child was actually 10. It was awkward for everyone. And it really matters, because a baby and a ten-year-old need completely different care, and maybe even a different nanny.

So now: each booking starts with a childcare brief that the parents and the nanny both check and confirm.

No clear policy

A sick child, and nobody knew what to do

On another booking the child got really sick and was throwing up. The parents asked me to leave and come back later. I'd already travelled there and kept that time free, and none of us knew what was fair. Nobody should have to work that out while a child is ill and everyone's stressed.

So now: there's a clear sick-child policy, and everyone sees it before they book.

Reserved dates

A trip that was cancelled right before we left

I'd planned my whole schedule around travelling with a family as their holiday nanny. It fell through just before I was meant to go. I understood, plans change. But those dates were gone, and I'd already said no to other work.

So now: cancellation terms are agreed up front, and they're fair to both sides.

Fair pay

Chasing money I'd already earned

I was once paid the wrong amount and had to keep pushing to get what I was owed. It's an awful feeling. No nanny should have to chase a family for money they've already worked for.

So now: payment goes through us, and the terms are clear before anyone commits.

The moment it clicked

"I could do this so much better."

When I finished my last nanny job, I couldn't shake that thought. I kept thinking about how much I love this work, and about everything parents had told me over the years.

So many of them had struggled to find someone they could trust with their kids. Websites that felt dodgy. Plans falling through at the last minute. Messages nobody answered. And I knew the other side too. Standing at a front door in a new place, about to meet a family I knew almost nothing about, and hoping it would go well.

I wanted to build something where parents can relax because they know exactly who's coming, and where nannies feel safe instead of nervous about meeting a new family. Something honest, properly checked, and run by someone who actually cares how it goes.

So I started Pro Travel Nannies.

What I'm building

Trust goes both ways.

Childcare only really works when the parents and the nanny can trust each other.

As a parent, especially in a country that isn't yours, you want to know exactly who's looking after your children. You want someone professional, honest answers, quick replies, and a real person to call if something goes wrong.

As a nanny, you need the same things back. The right details about the kids, clear hours, being paid properly and on time, and knowing that if you keep dates free for a family, they won't just disappear on you.

Parents should know who's looking after their children. Nannies should know who they're working for. And when something unexpected happens, both should have someone in their corner.

Because I've been the nanny

I know what it's like to walk into someone's home for the first time and just hope it goes well.

Because I was raised by a single mom

I watched my mom do it all on her own. I know what it means to a parent to have help they can actually rely on.

What I've built it on

Five things I won't budge on

Each of these comes from something I wish I'd had back when I was the one knocking on the door.

Verified

Families and nannies both get checked, and we show you exactly which checks have been done.

Matched

We match you based on your kids and what the nanny is good at, not on who happens to be free.

Personal

Real people with real photos and intro videos. You'll know who you're dealing with, and that includes me.

Protected

Booking terms, what happens if a child is sick, and cancellations are all agreed before anyone commits.

Supported

We don't introduce you and vanish. We're around from your first message until the last goodnight.

The company I wish existed

I want parents to feel like they've got a friend in a new country, and nannies to feel respected from the very first message. If I get those two things right, I'll be happy.

A personal note

I want you to know who's behind this.

I never wanted this to feel like a big, faceless agency. When you get in touch with Pro Travel Nannies, you're dealing with me. Someone who has been the nanny, and who has listened to a lot of parents tell me what worried them.

If you're a parent planning a trip, I'd love to help you find someone your kids will adore. If you're a nanny who has ever felt taken for granted, I'd really love for you to join us. Either way, you can always message me directly.
